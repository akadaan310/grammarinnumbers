# Abed Kadaan

**Founding Principal Researcher** of the research program to which this book belongs.

Abed Kadaan formulated the research question of *Grammar in Numbers* — *what is the computational grammar of number?* — and its relationship to his earlier publication, *Computational Grammar Theory*. He proposed the central experimental narrative of the book ($0 \to 1 \to 2 \to 3 \to 4$, then $1+1$, $0/1$, $1/0$, $0/0$, and $1 + 1 = 10_2$), the reading of $1/0$ and $0/0$ as two *different* grammatical failures, the segment notation $0\{1,2,3\}4$ as an object of investigation, the requirement that number, numeral, digit, machine word and abstract value be kept apart, and the governance rules of the publication: the ladder of claims, the independence of the two books, and honesty about novelty.

**Contributors.** The definitions, proofs, software, experiments and text of this edition were developed with Claude, an AI research assistant made by Anthropic, under the researcher's mandate. The contribution of each party to each result is recorded in the [provenance record](ledger/provenance.html).

**Affiliation and review.** No university appointment, institutional affiliation, endorsement or award is claimed. The book has not been peer reviewed; see the [publication status](status.html).

**The research program.** The program's first publication, [*Computational Grammar Theory: Structure, Operation, and the Cost of Resolution*](https://www.abedkadaan.com/books/computational-grammar-theory/), studies the grammars through which computational structures expose and resolve their operations. Both publications are presented together at [abedkadaan.com](https://www.abedkadaan.com/books/).
